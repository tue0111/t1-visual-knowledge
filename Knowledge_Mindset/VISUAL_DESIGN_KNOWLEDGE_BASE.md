## Think like a designer — general foundations and studio heuristics for image generation
### Single images · posters · photographs · product shots · editorial · illustration — mindset, structure, logic

# PART I — THE DESIGNER MINDSET

## 1.1 Purpose before pixels

Amateurs start with "what should it look like." Designers start with the brief. Before any visual word, answer five questions:

1. **MESSAGE** — what dominant, paired, polyphonic, or evolving relation must the image communicate? Default to one dominant goal for fast service work; keep several only when their relation is the brief.
2. **AUDIENCE** — who sees it, and what do they already believe/expect?
3. **CONTEXT** — where does it live? (billboard at 90 km/h, phone feed at 30 cm, gallery wall, package shelf, app icon at 16 px). Context dictates size of forms, contrast, and how much detail survives.
4. **FEELING** — the dominant, paired, deliberately ambivalent, or evolving emotional residue the viewer should carry away, as the brief requires.
5. **ACTION** — what should they do or remember afterwards?

A prompt that encodes these five answers *implicitly* through its choices beats a prompt stuffed with adjectives.

## 1.2 Studio heuristics of the trade

- **One dominant communication goal by default.** Polyphonic, ambivalent and narrative images may carry several ideas when their relation is the brief. Subtraction means removing what does not serve that relation.
- **Hierarchy is often job #1.** Distributed, patterned and contemplative work may use weaker or shifting hierarchy; declare the intended viewing behavior rather than forcing first/second/third everywhere.
- **Form follows function.** A style choice must be justified by the message ("why deco? why grain? why red?"). Unjustified styling reads as costume.
- **Constraints can support quality.** A limited palette, one light hierarchy or a fixed grid can make a direction testable; choose constraints that serve the problem. Fewer elements is one repair hypothesis, not a universal cure.
- **Multi-scale reading.** For quick communication, test glance, medium look and close inspection. Slow/contemplative work may deliberately delay meaning; use the brief's real time horizon.
- **Decoration vs communication.** Ornament is welcome when it *carries* the message (a luxury border says luxury); it is noise when it merely fills silence.

## 1.3 The designer's loop

`Brief → References → One-line concept → Rough (structure only) → Craft (light/color/detail) → Critique against the brief → Cut 20% → Ship.`
In prompt terms: never start typing render words before the concept line exists. A strong concept line: *"a lone red umbrella in a gray crowd, shot from above"* — subject, contrast mechanism, camera. Everything else is execution.

# PART II — VISUAL LOGIC: how images communicate

## 2.1 Six useful levers of hierarchy

Faces/eyes, local contrast, chroma difference, area, edge/detail and text can all attract attention, but their relative power changes with task, context, meaning and viewer expertise. Rank elements, then assign and test these levers at delivery size. If the hero is losing, first test muting the competing field (value, chroma, edge, detail or area) before automatically enlarging the hero.

## 2.2 Eye path

Design an **entry point**, a small set of likely transitions, a return point and a rest. Z, F, orbit and vector are candidate organizations, not guaranteed scanpaths; viewers saccade between salient and meaningful zones according to task. Gaze direction can be a strong cue, but it does not redirect every viewer identically.

## 2.3 Gestalt — the grammar of grouping

- **Proximity**: near things read as one group — control spacing to control meaning.
- **Similarity**: same color/shape = same team; the ONE dissimilar item becomes the hero for free (the red umbrella principle).
- **Closure**: viewers complete broken shapes — suggestion outperforms literal rendering (a rim-lit edge implies the whole body).
- **Continuity**: aligned things read as one line — misalignment reads as error, deliberate alignment reads as order.
- **Figure/ground**: the subject must separate from the field; when it doesn't, fix with value contrast, blur, or halo of negative space. Flip figure/ground deliberately for clever poster ideas.
- **Common region / enclosure**: a box, panel or vignette groups whatever it contains — frames-within-frames make instant hierarchy.

## 2.4 Contrast pairs — the engine of interest

Contrast pairs are useful design candidates: **big/small · light/dark · warm/cool · busy/empty · sharp/soft · organic/geometric · old/new · fast/still · precious/rough.** Pick one primary contrast and test whether it supports the message ("a tiny warm-lit figure in a vast cold empty hall"). Low-contrast or multi-contrast work can also succeed when the intended time horizon and hierarchy justify it.

## 2.5 Balance & visual weight

Weight comes from size, darkness, saturation, detail and "meaning" (faces weigh more). **Symmetric balance** = formal, iconic, sacred, still (center-composed portraits, temples, luxury). **Asymmetric balance** = dynamic, modern, editorial (a big quiet mass balanced by a small loud one — the 大小 principle). **Radial** = energy exploding from a hub (mandala, burst posters). Deliberate *imbalance* creates tension — use for unease, motion, drama.

# PART III — COMPOSITION SYSTEMS

## 3.1 Frame first

Choose the ratio for the medium and say it: **1:1** (icon, feed), **4:5** (portrait feed), **2:3 / 3:2** (print, classic photo), **16:9 / 21:9** (cinematic, banner), **9:16** (story/phone), **A-series vertical** (poster). The frame decides the composition family — verticals favor stacking and monumentality; wides favor journeys and horizons.

## 3.2 Placement systems (choose a dominant candidate)

- **Rule of thirds** — a convenient asymmetrical starting grid; test it against the subject, crop and intended tension. A centered horizon is valid when the structure needs it.
- **Dead center + symmetry** — iconic, confrontational, sacred or comedic-deadpan (Wes Anderson, religious icons, luxury bottles). Center demands symmetry discipline everywhere else.
- **Golden ratio / spiral** — a gentler thirds; use for elegant flowing subjects.
- **Diagonal / dynamic** — the baroque axis; energy, sports, action; let the subject cross the frame corner-to-corner.
- **Triangle** — group portraits and product families: one apex, stable base.
- **Frame-within-frame** — doorway, window, arch, hands; instant depth + instant focus.
- **Leading lines** — roads, rails, shadows, gazes converging on the hero.
- **Pattern & break** — repeat, repeat, repeat, INTERRUPT: the break is the message.

## 3.3 Depth — the three-layer rule

For coherent optical depth, coordinate **foreground · midground · background** with overlap, scale, perspective, atmosphere, edge and detail falloff. Deliberately flat, diagrammatic, symbolic and multi-view systems are equally valid when their spatial grammar is consistent. Mixed or contradictory depth cues may be used only when the contradiction serves the thesis.

## 3.4 Negative space & crop

Negative space is an active shape: it isolates the hero, gives text room, and signals confidence/luxury ("generous empty field around the subject"). **Cropping**: crop *into* a subject boldly (forehead crops feel intimate) but never at joints; leave **nose room / lead room** in the gaze or travel direction; tight crop = intensity, loose crop = context and calm.

# PART IV — LIGHT: the photographer's core weapon

## 4.1 Quality, direction, ratio

- **Quality** = source size relative to subject. Big & close = SOFT (beauty, calm, commerce); small & far = HARD (drama, grit, noon sun). Say it: "soft wrapping window light" vs "hard single spotlight with crisp shadows."
- **Direction** speaks: **front** (flat, honest, fashion-clean), **45° side** (form and character — the default), **full side/split** (duality, drama), **back/rim** (silhouette, mystery, halo — the cheapest epic there is), **top** (interrogation, holiness), **under** (horror, villainy).
- **Ratio**: **high-key** (bright, minimal shadows — optimism, healthcare, tech) vs **low-key** (dark field, sculpted highlights — luxury, noir, power). **Chiaroscuro** = the painterly extreme.

## 4.2 Named setups (portrait vocabulary the models know)

**Rembrandt** (45° key, triangle of light on the far cheek — classic character), **butterfly/paramount** (frontal high key, shadow under nose — glamour), **loop** (small nose-shadow loop — friendly standard), **split** (half-face lit — duality), **rim/edge** (light from behind outlines the figure), **clamshell** (soft key above + fill below — beauty ads), **silhouette** (expose for the bright background). One key light + one job; every extra light needs a reason (fill, rim, background separation, practical).

## 4.3 Time, weather, world light

**Golden hour** (long warm soft — romance, nostalgia), **blue hour** (cool ambient + warm windows — cinematic melancholy), **overcast** (giant softbox — color fidelity, quiet), **harsh noon** (brutal graphic shadows — fashion-edgy or documentary-honest), **night neon** (mixed color practicals — cyber, urban loneliness), **candle/fire** (intimate flicker), **moonlight** (blue, low ratio). **Motivated light** — every light in frame should have a plausible source (window, lamp, sign); unmotivated glow reads AI-fake fastest of all.

## 4.4 Color of light

State temperature and mixing in relation to the motivated sources. "Warm tungsten key against cool dusk fill" is one common cinematic heuristic, not a universal recipe. One, two or more source temperatures can work when source hierarchy, adaptation and material response remain coherent.

# PART V — COLOR AS A SYSTEM

- **Test value architecture.** A grayscale study is useful for mass and focal separation, but perceived lightness remains context-dependent and color may carry structure that grayscale removes.
- **Harmony schemes**: monochrome, analogous, complementary, split-complementary and triadic are palette vocabularies. Treat their emotional and harmony claims as hypotheses; test color in its actual surround, area ratio and illumination.
- **60-30-10**: use it only as a starting distribution when dominant/secondary/accent roles are useful. Salience is not proportional to area, and the most chromatic region is not automatically the semantic message.
- **Temperature relationship**: warm-on-cool can separate a subject in many contexts, but warm does not always advance and cool does not always recede; value, surround, atmosphere, area and meaning can reverse the effect.
- **Palette archetypes** (use as starting points): corporate trust (deep blue + gray + white), luxury (black + gold + one jewel), organic/eco (sage, cream, earth), retro-pastel (mint, blush, butter, 70s ochre), neon-cyber (magenta/cyan on near-black), Mediterranean travel (cobalt + white + terracotta), editorial minimal (cream, black, one red), candy-pop (saturated primaries on white).
- **Color semantics, with a hard caveat**: any red/blue/green/yellow/purple/black/white association depends on objects, events, culture, era, illumination and audience. Verify rather than importing a color-psychology chart.
- **Discipline clauses are conditional**: a restrained palette, neutral balance, reserved saturation or no rainbow gradient can repair specific drift; they are not mandatory for every art system.

# PART VI — PHOTOGRAPHY LANGUAGE (the vocabulary models obey)

## 6.1 Camera position

**Shot scale**: extreme wide (world), wide (place), full (body), medium (waist-up, conversation), close-up (face, emotion), extreme close-up / macro (texture, obsession). **Angle**: eye-level (neutral, honest), low angle (power, monument), high angle (vulnerability, overview), overhead/top-down (graphic, flat-lay, fate), dutch tilt (unease — use rarely), worm's eye (awe).

## 6.2 Lens grammar

- **14–20 mm ultra-wide**: exaggerated foreground, bending drama, interiors; distorts faces (only for intent).
- **24–35 mm**: environmental storytelling — subject IN a place; street standard.
- **50 mm full-frame equivalent**: a common normal-lens starting field of view; the eye has no single equivalent focal length, and framing/perspective still depend on format and camera position.
- **85–135 mm**: portrait compression — flattering faces, melted backgrounds, isolation.
- **200 mm+**: stacked compression (crowds, traffic, mountains piled on the subject); surveillance distance.
- **Macro**: the invisible world; **tilt-shift**: miniature toy effect or corrected architecture.
- **Aperture**: f/1.2–f/2 = razor focus plane + creamy bokeh (isolation, intimacy); f/8–f/16 = everything sharp (documentary, landscape, product). Say "shallow depth of field, background melted to bokeh" or "deep focus, sharp front to back."
- **Shutter**: frozen droplets (1/4000), motion-blur streaks, panning (sharp subject, streaked world), long-exposure silk water / light trails / star trails.

## 6.3 Process & stock (instant mood)

35mm grain (Portra warm skin, Ektachrome cool pop, Cinestill halation-red night), Tri-X gritty B&W, large-format 8×10 (high resolution + controllable plane), Polaroid (soft, bordered, nostalgic), cross-process, cyanotype, wet-plate collodion, infrared (white foliage), drone GPS-view. "Shot on [stock/format]" can be a dense generator cue, but its effect is model/version-dependent and should be decoded into visible behavior for production.

## 6.4 Genre recipes (compressed)

- **Portrait**: 85 mm, f/2, Rembrandt or window light, quiet background 2 stops darker, catchlight in the eyes, "sharp eyes" stated.
- **Editorial fashion**: hard flash or noon sun, bold single-color field or brutalist location, stiff-elegant pose, negative space for type.
- **Product / packshot**: seamless sweep or context vignette, big soft key + rim for edges, controlled reflections on glass/metal ("clean specular highlights"), the product's *material* named (matte, brushed, glossy).
- **Food**: directional back-side window light is a common texture-revealing recipe; frontal, flat, cool, hard or deep-focus food work is valid when the concept/product requires it.
- **Architecture**: verticals kept vertical ("corrected perspective"), dawn/dusk with interior lights on, one-point or two-point stated.
- **Landscape**: three layers + scale figure, golden/blue hour, f/11, leading line to the light.
- **Street**: 35 mm, decisive-moment verb, layered fore/mid/background, imperfect and alive.
- **Still life**: painterly single-source chiaroscuro, dark field, arranged triangle, vanitas props.

# PART VII — GRAPHIC DESIGN & TYPOGRAPHY (posters, layouts, covers)

## 7.1 Grid thinking

Even a generated poster needs an invisible skeleton: **margins** (breathing room — cheap posters bleed to the edge, luxury floats), **alignment** (everything aligns with *something*; one axis of alignment minimum), **column logic** for text zones, and **whitespace as the luxury signal**. Prompt terms: "clean grid layout, generous margins, strong alignment, ample negative space reserved for text."

## 7.2 Type as a voice (and the AI caveat)

Type hierarchy may use display → subhead → body → caption with clearly distinguishable roles; exact ratios are context-dependent. Typeface associations are cultural/category heuristics, not fixed meanings. Contrast pairing is a reliable starting point, while similar fonts can work when their subtle distinction and hierarchy are intentional.
**AI reality:** generated text garbles. Strategy: (a) keep in-image words ≤1–2 short words if essential, (b) otherwise prompt "bold clear areas of negative space reserved for typography, no lettering" and set type in post — this is how professionals use generated key art anyway.

## 7.3 Poster genres (recipes)

- **Swiss / International**: grid, one photo or geometric form, grotesque type, red/black/white, ruthless hierarchy.
- **Movie key art**: one dominant figure or duel of faces, layered montage behind, title zone at lower third, strong single color grade.
- **Retro travel**: flat illustration, 3-color separation, big landmark simplified, sunburst sky, deco/hand-lettered title zone.
- **Constructivist / propaganda**: diagonal composition, red-black-cream, photomontage + halftone, upward heroic angles.
- **Music gig**: one weird striking image, texture (riso/screenprint), all type hand-cut energy.
- **Minimalist concept**: one object recontextualized as metaphor, vast empty field, tiny title. (The hardest and most premium.)
- **Japanese contemporary**: playful asymmetry, huge type as image, pastel + one clash.
- **Brutalist**: raw, system-default aesthetics, harsh crops — anti-design as design.

## 7.4 Identity thinking (logos, icons, covers)

Identity marks can be tested as silhouettes at their **actual minimum size**; 16 px is only one icon scenario. One concept, flat-first and one-second cover reading are useful starting constraints, not laws: layered metaphors, gradients and slow covers may succeed when the brief and reproduction context support them. Always check on the real phone, shelf, feed, print or sign.

# PART VIII — MEDIUM & STYLE SELECTOR (single image)

Three dials position ANY image:

1. **Medium**: photograph ↔ painting ↔ illustration ↔ print ↔ 3D render ↔ collage. Name the medium first token ("editorial photograph", "matte gouache illustration", "risograph print"). Unnamed medium = mushy hybrid.
2. **Realism dial**: photoreal → stylized-real → graphic → abstract. Consistency along the dial matters more than the position: half-real faces on graphic bodies is the uncanny zone.
3. **Era/process dial**: contemporary-digital ↔ any historical process (oil, woodblock, daguerreotype, 80s airbrush, Y2K chrome…). Era words are dense style-packages — one era at a time.

**Fusion starting pattern:** take structure from one source and surface/process from another, then state jurisdiction. This is one `structural graft` mode, not a universal fusion law. For split-domain, process translation, productive tension and cultural context, use `ART_FUSION_AND_CREATIVE_SYNTHESIS.md`.

# PART IX — THE SINGLE-IMAGE PROMPT FORMULA

## 9.1 The stack (order matters)

```
1. MEDIUM + FRAME     "Editorial photograph, 4:5"  /  "Flat vector poster, A2 vertical"
2. SUBJECT + ACTION   one hero, one verb, concrete nouns ("a ceramicist's hands centering wet clay")
3. CONTEXT            environment, era, weather — only what serves the message
4. COMPOSITION        placement system + depth layers + negative space intention
5. LIGHT              quality + direction + time/setup name
6. COLOR              scheme + palette (3-5 named colors) + where saturation lives
7. STYLE / PROCESS    lens/stock or art movement/artist-adjacent language or print process
8. CRAFT FLAGS        "sharp eyes", "clean specular highlights", "corrected verticals", texture notes
9. NEGATIVES          the known failure modes of this genre (below)
```

Prompt weighting, capitalization and positional effects are generator/version hypotheses. Put load-bearing intent and structure early for readability, but verify actual obedience through controlled samples; do not treat a fixed word count as causal proof.

## 9.2 Worked micro-examples

- *Portrait:* "Editorial portrait photograph, 4:5. An elderly luthier in his workshop, half-turned toward a window. 85mm, f/2, shallow focus, sharp eyes. Rembrandt window light, dust motes; background workbench melted to warm bokeh, 2 stops darker. Palette: walnut brown, brass, one deep green apron; muted, neutral balance. Shot on Portra 400, soft grain. Negative: plastic skin, HDR glow, oversaturation, busy background, extra hands."
- *Poster:* "Minimalist concept poster, A2 vertical. A single lit match standing in a field of unlit matches, top-down. Swiss grid, subject at the golden-ratio point, vast smoke-gray negative space above reserved for typography, no lettering. Hard single spotlight, long shadow. Palette: charcoal, cream, one vermilion flame — saturation only on the flame. Negative: text, watermark, clutter, gradients, reflections."
- *Product:* "Studio product photograph, 1:1. A matte-black espresso machine on a slate plinth, three-quarter view. Deep focus f/11. Large soft key from upper left, crisp rim light tracing the right edge, controlled specular line on the chrome lever. Charcoal seamless background with a subtle floor reflection. Palette: black, slate, one amber cup as accent. Negative: blown highlights, warped geometry, floating product, busy reflections, text."

## 9.3 Iteration protocol

Change **one causal hypothesis per regeneration** and log every coupled field it requires. A light hypothesis may legitimately change source, shadow and material highlight together; a camera hypothesis may couple distance, perspective and anatomy. A/B against the brief, not against "prettier." Preserve winning decisions when the system is sound; reframe the concept when repeated outputs fail the core intent rather than relying on a fixed percentage threshold.

Before writing the next instruction, keep a three-field diagnosis ledger:

```yaml
preserve: successful identity, hierarchy, composition, light, material, or mood
change: one observable failure and one target state
uncertain: sampling variance, ambiguous wording, reference contamination, or possible tool mismatch
```

The `uncertain` field prevents random prompt inflation. Re-sample when the direction is sound but variance is plausible; rewrite when the instruction is ambiguous; repartition references when face, wardrobe, and style bleed together; change tools only when the required capability is outside the current workflow. Teach with failure-to-repair sequences, not only finished galleries: the sequence exposes what failed, what changed, and why the repair may transfer.

## 9.4 Error taxonomy — single images

| Symptom | Cause | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| No focal point / wallpaper | no hierarchy decided | pick ONE hero; mute everything else (blur/desat/darken) |
| Overcooked HDR look | "epic, ultra-detailed" stacking | one light source, named film stock, "restrained, natural" |
| Plastic skin / doll face | beauty-word overdose | "natural skin texture, visible pores, film grain", real stock names |
| Sticker-composite subject | subject/scene lit separately | one named light shared: "lit by the same window light, contact shadow" |
| Kitsch saturation | unbounded palette roles | define dominant/support/accent/neutral roles and test them in context |
| Unintended static balance | placement does not serve intent | test asymmetry, direction or spacing; preserve center when it is purposeful |
| Garbled text | asking AI to typeset | reserve blank type zones; ≤2 short words max; post-production |
| Amber/teal slop grade | trend prior | "neutral white balance", name the exact palette |
| Uncanny half-real style | ontology/medium boundary inconsistent | one grammar per render domain plus an authored relationship; add a physical bridge when shared-world integration is intended |
| Fake floating glow | optical cause or expressive function is unstated | name a physical source when optical belief is intended, or declare the graphic/symbolic role when it is not |
| Warped products/architecture | perspective unspecified | "corrected verticals, straight geometry, one-point perspective" |
| Generic "AI look" | no medium/era/process token | name a stock, a process, a movement — commit to one |

## 9.5 Final QA — four tests before shipping

1. **Squint test**: blur your eyes — does the hierarchy survive as 3 blobs in the right order?
2. **Grayscale test**: would it read with color removed?
3. **Time-horizon test**: does the intended first read land at the speed required by the brief, and does delayed meaning survive when contemplative reading is intended?
4. **Context test**: drop it into its real home (feed, wall, shelf) at real size — does it still win?

## CODA — ten operating heuristics

1. Give the image a dominant communication relation unless the brief deliberately requires polyphony.
2. Answer the brief's five questions before typing a single visual word.
3. Subtract until it hurts, then stop — taste is what you leave out.
4. Design entry, return and discovery for the brief's real viewing time.
5. Choose a dominant spatial organization, contrast relationship and light hierarchy, then test them.
6. Establish mass/value architecture and color roles; salience must serve semantic importance.
7. Every light has a declared physical, graphic or symbolic logic; every style choice has a reason.
8. Name the medium, the process and the frame — vagueness is the enemy.
9. Test one causal hypothesis at a time; log every coupled field and acceptance criterion.
10. Judge against the brief, never against "prettier."
