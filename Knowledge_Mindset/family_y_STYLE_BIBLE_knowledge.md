# COMIC WORLD & STYLE BIBLE — Mindset & Workflow
## How each style *sees and builds its whole world*

> **What this file is.** Not a general art-theory dump, and not a doc about two characters. It captures, for each style, the **worldview** — the kind of *universe* that style imagines and the concrete way it builds space, architecture, light, material, atmosphere, mood and camera. The two leads are just the travelers who walk through every one of these worlds; they get one short shared section near the end.
>
> **What it excludes.** Story beats and any interior / anatomy imagery — those live in the story masters. "World" here means the style's *way of seeing* and its exterior settings (night, hall, doors, space, air), described as visual language, never as plot. Ultra-Realistic is excluded by request.
>
> **How to use it.** Read a style's profile to get inside its head before you design *any* scene in it. Then use the world-first workflow in §4 and paste the style's world-building block into the prompt.
>
> **2026 scope note:** this file is a casebook for a particular comic/world system. Its neutral-balance, gold-locality, unified-medium and world-first clauses are family/project preferences, not universal art laws. Begin with the current brief; use `00_FORMATION_ROUTER.md` for general fine-arts, fusion and critique knowledge.

---

## 0. The lens — the questions every style answers about its world

A "style" here is not a filter. It is a **set of decisions about what kind of world exists** and how it is shown. Every profile below is really an answer to these seven world-questions:

1. **What kind of universe is this?** (mythic, noir, grounded, ceremonial, epic-empty, nostalgic…) — the emotional register of the *place*, before any character enters.
2. **How is space built?** Through solid architecture, or through negative space and silhouette, or through atmospheric depth? How does scale make you feel small or grand?
3. **What is the light logic?** One dramatic key light, flat graphic shadow, noir masses, or even naturalistic tone? Light is what makes a world *feel* like something.
4. **Is the world one substance, or two tiers?** Are the inhabitants made of the same "matter" as the world (unified), or is a stylized guest dropped into a more richly rendered world (two-tier)? This is the single biggest world decision in this family.
5. **Ornament or restraint?** Does the world *want* decorative geometry and framing, or does it want emptiness and silence?
6. **Camera & staging.** Is it composed like a film, a design poster, an animation cel, or a manga page? Where does the reader's eye stand inside the world?
7. **Texture density.** Is the world crisp and low-noise, or richly detailed and atmospheric? Where is the detail budget spent?

**One project-specific guard holds across these nine worlds — color discipline.** For this casebook, neutral white balance prevents the observed global amber/gold/sepia collapse; gold stays local and the two costume colors do not tint the world. Other projects may intentionally use colored adaptation or global grading.

## 1. The nine worlds

Each profile leads with **the world it builds**, then how that world handles **space & scale**, **light & atmosphere**, **material & texture**, and **color as atmosphere** — and only then how **figures sit inside it**. The copy-paste **world-building block** at the end of each carries environment/space/light/mood lines, not just character lines.

### FAMILY A — Cinematic / painterly worlds
*Worlds made of light and air. You feel the atmosphere before you read a shape.*

---

#### A1 · Cinematic Chinese-Xianxia Manhwa — "the world lit by one light"

**The world it builds.** An intimate, moody, filmic universe where **everything belongs to the same breathable air**. It is not a bright storybook and not a hard graphic poster — it is a cinematic stage at night, softened by haze, where forms emerge from shadow. The founding worldview is *one light, one atmosphere, one material*: the whole world is painted in a single language, so it reads as a real, tangible, slightly dreamlike place rather than a set of drawn objects.

**Space & scale.** Depth is built by **overlapping lit forms receding into shadow**, not by hard perspective lines. Large areas fall into rich dark; the eye travels from the lit focal subject back into gloom. Space feels deep and enclosing, atmospheric rather than architectural.

**Light & atmosphere — A1 case rule.** For this family, one dominant key light per panel (a lantern, a shaft of light) carves the scene; deep controlled shadow shapes and gentle rim light separate the subject. Its intended mood is carried primarily by value contrast while colored tint remains local; another family may legitimately use global chromatic adaptation. Let shadow hide detail where this family's hierarchy calls for it.

**Material & texture.** Painterly digital brushwork; forms defined by light and shadow more than by outline. Controlled, not sketchy, not noisy. Everything — stone, cloth, air — shares this one painted substance.

**Color as atmosphere.** Deep neutral indigo-black, cool blue-grey stone, muted jade/teal, and **one local warm glow** that stays local. Deliberately neutral balance so the world never yellows.

**Figures within it.** Painted *into* the scene with the same light and material — never a cutout laid on top.

```text
[STYLE_HEADER]
Cinematic Chinese-xianxia webtoon / manhwa comic page, 2:3 vertical, premium graphic-novel quality. A moody filmic WORLD built from light and atmosphere: ONE unified painterly material for the entire scene — architecture, air, props and figures all painted in the same language, lit by the same light, sitting in the same haze; nothing is a flat sticker over a painting. Forms emerge from light and shadow more than from hard outline. Deep atmospheric depth; cinematic composition; clear black gutters. NOT flat, NOT minimalist, NOT low-poly, NOT photoreal, NOT 3D, NOT chibi.
[WORLD_LIGHT] The world is defined by ONE dominant key light per panel (lantern, shaft of interior light). Deep controlled shadow masses; large areas fall into rich dark; gentle rim light separates subject from ground. Depth = overlapping lit forms receding into shadow. Let SHADOW hide detail; render fully only where light lands. Mood from value contrast, never a color tint.
[WORLD_ATMOSPHERE] Neutral white balance: a pale element reads clean-neutral, not cream/gold. No global amber/gold/sepia wash, no warm filter over frame, no yellow bloom. Palette: deep neutral indigo-black, cool blue-grey stone, muted jade/teal, ONE local warm glow that stays local. Atmospheric haze, filmic depth of field.
[FIGURES_IN_WORLD] All figures are painted INTO the world with the same brush material and the same light — softly shaded, rim-lit, sitting in the haze, never a flat cutout on top.
Negative: global amber wash, gold cast, sepia, warm filter over frame, flat cartoon character, pasted sticker character, cutout on painted background, character not matching scene light, chibi, anime big eyes, photoreal skin, 3D render, in-panel text, garbled letters, watermark, cross-panel background leakage
```

#### A2 · Cinematic Art-Deco Xianwuxia — "the myth staged as design"

**The world it builds.** A **heightened, ceremonial xianxia myth-world** where reality is stylized into **bold Art-Deco graphic design**. This universe is not merely lit — it is *composed*. Nature becomes geometry (bamboo as pattern, the moon as a disc), architecture becomes silhouette and framing, the night becomes teal-and-gold ceremony. It is a legendary, glamorous, self-aware graphic-novel cosmos: an imperial fantasy staged like a poster and narrated with comic lettering.

**Space & scale.** Space is arranged as **design** as much as architecture — symmetry, strong silhouettes, deco framing, a central subject against a stylized backdrop (a round lantern / moon disc behind the figure). Panel *shape* itself builds the world: narrow verticals for descent, round panels for reflection, borderless bleeds for grandeur, splash pages for spectacle.

**Light & atmosphere.** Cinematic low-key light and atmospheric haze inherited from A1, but pushed toward **drama and glamour** — high contrast, gold key light as ceremony. The world feels mythic and theatrical.

**Material & texture.** Two layers by design: **glossy semi-real painted matter** for figures and key surfaces, plus **flat graphic Art-Deco shapes** for ornament and silhouette. Elegant, never cluttered; the deco is strongest on portrait, silhouette and transition beats.

**Color as atmosphere.** Teal-and-gold: ink-black, deep blue-teal, jade, with warm gold/amber lantern glow as a dramatic *key* (graded warmth welcome; no flat muddy yellow).

**Figures within it.** Semi-real xianxia figures framed by deco backdrops and rim light — legends posed inside a designed world.

```text
[STYLE_HEADER]
Cinematic Art-Deco xianxia / wuxia webtoon graphic-novel page, 2:3 vertical, premium quality. A heightened ceremonial MYTH-WORLD where reality is stylized into bold Art-Deco graphic design: nature becomes geometry (bamboo pattern, round lantern / moon discs), architecture becomes silhouette and deco framing, night becomes a teal-and-warm-gold ceremony. Glossy SEMI-REALISTIC figure painting FUSED with flat graphic deco shapes; cinematic low-key lighting, atmospheric haze, high contrast; Western comic lettering layered on (balloons, caption boxes, bold English SFX). NOT flat cartoon, NOT ultra-photoreal, NOT manga screentone, NOT 3D, NOT chibi.
[WORLD_AS_DESIGN] Space is COMPOSED, not just lit: symmetry, bold silhouettes, restrained deco framing, a central subject against a stylized backdrop (round lantern / moon disc). Panel SHAPE builds the world — narrow verticals for descent, round panels for reflection, borderless bleeds for grandeur, full-page splash for spectacle. Deco strongest on portrait / silhouette / transition beats; elegant, never cluttered.
[WORLD_LIGHT_COLOR] Cinematic teal-and-gold: ink-black, deep blue-teal, jade green, warm gold/amber lantern glow as a dramatic KEY (graded warmth welcome; no flat muddy uniform yellow). High contrast, moody, theatrical, mythic.
[TEXT_SYSTEM] English ASCII lettering is part of this world: short balloons (<=6 words), caption boxes, bold uppercase SFX (<=8 letters). English ASCII only. If any text renders garbled, leave the balloon EMPTY and add in post. Small page-number box bottom-right.
Negative: flat cartoon people, chibi, big moe eyes, manga screentone, ultra-photoreal plastic skin, 3D render, muddy uniform yellow wash, Chinese text in image, garbled text, watermark, distorted face, extra fingers, wukong clones, cross-panel background leakage
```

#### A3 · Cinematic Organ Atlas — "the world as a curated gallery"

**The world it builds.** The Xianwuxia myth-world seen through a **curatorial, exhibition mindset**. This universe treats its own contents as **specimens in a premium illustrated codex** — presented under gallery light, richly detailed, and *annotated* with elegant bilingual Art-Deco plaques. The worldview is reverent and scholarly: the world is something to be *displayed, named and marveled at*, like a high-end anatomical-fantasy art book. (Here "atlas" is a presentation attitude — museum-grade craft and labeling — not a change of subject.)

**Space & scale.** Space becomes **display space**: a hero subject centered and lit like an exhibit, with room reserved for label cards. Fewer, larger, more immersive compositions than the action-dense hall pages.

**Light & atmosphere.** Volumetric, atmospheric gallery light with shallow depth of field — the same cinematic teal-and-gold grade as Xianwuxia, tuned for a hushed, high-production, exhibition hush.

**Material & texture.** The signature is a **higher realism-and-detail budget on the environment** (hyperreal, atmospheric, fine surface detail) beneath the glossy semi-real figures — plus a dedicated **typographic layer**: dark-teal deco **label cards** with thin gold leader lines, a name in large Chinese over smaller English, one short English description; optionally a corner **region title**. Use 2–4 cards per page, elegantly placed, never covering a figure. This bilingual deco-label treatment is the whole identity of the style.

**Color as atmosphere.** Teal-and-gold plus the label-card color: dark-teal plaques and gold flourishes threaded through the scene.

**Figures within it.** Same semi-real leads as Xianwuxia — the atlas attitude changes the *presentation and annotation* of the world, not the cast.

```text
[STYLE_HEADER]
Cinematic REALISTIC atlas x Art-Deco xianxia graphic-novel page, 2:3 vertical, premium GALLERY quality. A curated exhibition WORLD: the environment is presented like a specimen in a high-end illustrated codex — a HIGH realism-and-detail budget (hyperreal, atmospheric, fine surface detail, volumetric cinematic light), glossy SEMI-REALISTIC figures, elegant Art-Deco framing, a teal-and-warm-gold palette, and beautiful BILINGUAL (Chinese + English) Art-Deco LABEL CARDS. Reverent, museum-grade, high production value. NOT flat cartoon, NOT chibi, NOT 3D-plastic, NOT manga screentone.
[WORLD_AS_GALLERY] Compose space as DISPLAY space: a hero subject centered and lit like an exhibit, fewer/larger immersive compositions, room reserved for label cards. Shallow depth of field; hushed high-production atmosphere.
[LABEL_CARDS] Elegant Art-Deco label cards annotate the world: a small dark-teal deco-framed plaque + thin gold leader line + a name in CHINESE (large) over ENGLISH (below) + one short English description line; optionally a decorative Chinese REGION TITLE with a small English subtitle in a corner. 2-4 cards per page max, elegantly placed, NEVER covering the figure. Names correct. Garbled text -> keep the card empty, add in post. This bilingual deco-label treatment is the signature.
[WORLD_LIGHT_COLOR] Cinematic teal-and-gold: ink-black, deep blue-teal, jade green, warm gold/amber glow (graded warmth; no flat muddy yellow); label cards in dark teal + gold. High contrast, moody, atmospheric.
Negative: flat cartoon, cel-shaded flat surfaces, low-poly, faceted, papercraft, chibi, big moe eyes, manga screentone, 3D-plastic render, cluttered labels covering the figure, wrong or misspelled labels, muddy uniform yellow wash, garbled text, watermark, distorted face, extra fingers, wukong clones, cross-panel background leakage
```

### FAMILY B — Dark-Deco flat / animation worlds
*Worlds made of bold shapes, hard flat shadow and deliberate emptiness. Design IS the place.*

---

#### B1 · Dark-Deco Cartoon Network — "the epic-empty graphic world"

**The world it builds.** A **Tartakovsky (Samurai Jack / Primal) universe**: monumental, mythic and *mostly empty*. The worldview is scale-through-silence — a world of a few enormous simple shapes and vast negative space, where a small figure stands against a huge flat plane and the emptiness itself is the drama. Cool, epic, a little ominous, timeless.

**Space & scale.** Built from **silhouette, flat color planes and dramatic scale contrast**. Architecture is monolithic and geometric; backgrounds reduce to a few bold shapes; **generous negative space** is a feature, not a gap. Design *is* the environment.

**Light & atmosphere.** **Hard-edged FLAT shadow shapes** — no gradients, no rendering. High graphic contrast, animation-cel crisp. The mood is carved by where the big flat darks fall.

**Material & texture.** One unified flat bold language for the whole world — thick clean edges, flat fills, minimal texture. Nothing looks pasted because everything is the same graphic substance. Low-detail on purpose (efficient and stable).

**Color as atmosphere.** A limited dark-deco palette — ink-black, deep teal/jade, indigo night, cool stone grey — with gold as a controlled local accent only.

**Figures within it.** Bold flat angular characters unified with the world and lit by the same flat graphic shadow, dwarfed by the big shapes around them.

```text
[STYLE_HEADER]
Dark-Deco Cartoon-Network animation WORLD (in the vein of Genndy Tartakovsky's Samurai Jack / Primal): monumental and mostly EMPTY — a few enormous simple graphic shapes, generous negative space, dramatic scale contrast, angular Art-Deco geometry, a limited flat palette, hard-edged FLAT shadow shapes, high contrast, minimal texture. ONE unified flat bold animated language for the WHOLE world so nothing looks pasted. Cool, epic, timeless, faintly ominous. NOT photoreal, NOT 3D, NOT painterly, NOT manga/screentone, NOT ultra-detailed realism, NOT chibi.
[WORLD_SPACE] Build space from silhouette, flat color planes and scale contrast: monolithic geometric architecture, backgrounds reduced to a few bold shapes, generous negative space as a FEATURE, a small figure against a huge flat plane. Design IS the environment.
[WORLD_LIGHT] Hard-edged FLAT shadow shapes only — no gradients, no rendering, no hatching. High graphic contrast, animation-cel crisp. Mood carved by where the big flat darks fall.
[WORLD_COLOR] Limited dark-deco palette: ink-black, deep teal / jade green, indigo night, cool stone grey, with GOLD / warm amber as controlled LOCAL accents only (never a muddy global yellow).
Negative: photoreal, 3D render, painterly, watercolor, manga, screentone, hatching, ultra-detailed realism, soft gradient, realistic skin, plastic doll, chibi, big moe eyes, cluttered ornamental frame, muddy yellow wash, garbled text, watermark, distorted face, extra fingers, wukong clones, cross-panel background leakage
```

#### B2 · Dark-Deco 1990s Retro Cartoon — "the timeless noir-deco serial"

**The world it builds.** A **Bruce Timm "Dark Deco" universe** (Batman: The Animated Series) crossed with Samurai Jack: a **retro, film-noir, nostalgic** world that feels like a timeless animated serial from an era that never quite existed. Where B1 is cool and epic-empty, B2 is **romantic and shadowy** — streamlined deco architecture, dramatic noir mood, a warmer, aged palette. The universe feels hand-inked on cels and lit like 1940s cinema.

**Space & scale.** Bold graphic staging with **strong Art-Deco composition** — streamlined, symmetrical architecture and moody deco backgrounds. Space reads through dramatic **noir shadow shapes** cutting across the frame, and clean black gutters.

**Light & atmosphere.** **1–2 tone hard-edged flat shadow**, dramatic noir key light, deep moody darks. Optional very light film grain gives the vintage, timeless serial feel.

**Material & texture.** Pure retro cel: thick clean confident outlines, flat cel fills, strong silhouettes. One unified retro-cel substance for the whole world.

**Color as atmosphere.** A limited **retro** palette: deep desaturated teal / blue-grey, ink-black, warm ochre / mustard, muted brick-red, cream — warmer and more nostalgic than B1's cool graphics.

**Figures within it.** Bold flat retro-cel characters, elegant and angular, lit by the same noir shadow as the world.

#### B3 · Modern Dark-Deco DC Comic — "the serious inked world with a cartoon guest"

**The world it builds.** A **prestige modern DC / Vertigo graphic-novel universe** — mature, weighty, atmospheric, carried by ink and noir light. This is the family's defining **two-tier** world: the *world itself* is the serious, richly rendered layer with real architectural heft and gloom, and a **flat cartoon character is deliberately dropped into it as a guest**. The tension between a grave inked reality and a bright toon is the whole concept. The universe feels grand, shadowed, gold-accented and designed like a premium page.

**Space & scale.** Real architectural weight and hazy depth; streamlined symmetrical architecture as backdrop; big impact panels, insets and full-page splashes. The world is framed like a designed object — **thin gold Art-Deco borders** and restrained corner motifs are part of the place.

**Light & atmosphere.** **Hard noir key light with deep, shaped shadow masses**; Art-Deco geometry echoed *in the shadow shapes*; gold rim light on edges; atmospheric haze. Shadow carries the mood and hides clutter.

**Material & texture.** Bold clean digital ink with variable-width line art for the world and its serious inhabitants — **richly inked, cinematically rendered**. The single toon guest stays flat-cel *by design* but still catches the scene's cast shadow so he occupies the space.

**Color as atmosphere.** Ink-black, deep teal/jade, indigo night, cool stone grey, with gold and warm amber as controlled local accents (lanterns, deco borders, rim light) — never a muddy uniform yellow.

**Figures within it.** The world and its serious figures are one richly-inked tier; the cartoon lead is the intentional other tier — a toon walking through a grave, beautiful, deco-framed reality.

```text
[STYLE_HEADER]
Modern Dark-Deco DC-comic graphic-novel WORLD, 2:3 vertical, premium quality. #### B4 · Dark-Deco Minimalism — "the world reduced to a poster"
```

**The world it builds.** A **reductive, elegant, quiet universe** defined by what is *left out*. The mindset is restraint: the world is suggested by the fewest essential shapes and a great deal of negative space, like a refined Deco poster. Built originally to kill noise and instability, its worldview is clarity and calm — a place that breathes because it is nearly empty.

**Space & scale.** **Negative space and a couple of graphic planes** are the environment. Backgrounds reduce to a few shapes; one focal subject per panel; low spatial frequency. Scale and mood come from emptiness and a single accent.

**Light & atmosphere.** Graphic (not photographic) light: simple hard-edged shadow shapes, clean pale highlights. **Neutral white balance is the first commandment** — this world exists specifically to have no amber cast.

**Material & texture.** Flat minimalist poster matter for the whole exterior world — bold clean black-ink linework, large flat color areas, minimal texture. A realistic detail tier is held back and spent only on the single most important focal surface.

**Color as atmosphere.** Ink-black, deep indigo, blue-grey stone, muted silver, cool neutral night — flat and calm; highlights clean and pale, never yellow.

**Figures within it.** Flat minimalist figures inside or out, reading as clean silhouettes against the empty world — dignified, never cheapened by the simplification.

```text
[STYLE_HEADER]
Dark-Deco Minimalism comic WORLD, 2:3 vertical — a reductive, elegant, quiet universe defined by what is LEFT OUT. Flat Dark-Deco poster world: bold clean black-ink linework, few large flat shapes, strong silhouettes, limited flat color, generous negative space, minimal texture, poster-like simplicity, low detail; shadows are simple hard-edged shapes; backgrounds reduced to a few graphic planes; graphic (not photographic) lighting. A held-back realistic detail tier is spent only on the single most important focal surface. Neutral white balance on everything. Calm, clear, elegant.
[WORLD_RESTRAINT] The environment IS negative space plus a couple of graphic planes. ONE focal subject per panel, ONE spatial relationship, large clean shapes, low spatial frequency, simplified/empty backgrounds, readable silhouettes above all. Scale and mood come from emptiness and a single accent. No fine speckle, no crowded detail. No generated text, labels, captions, logo or watermark.
[WORLD_BALANCE] Neutral white balance across the whole world: no global amber/yellow/sepia wash, no localized yellow bloom, no warm filter over the frame. Highlights clean and pale (near-neutral), never yellow. Palette: ink-black, deep indigo, blue-grey stone, muted silver, cool neutral night — flat and calm.
```

### FAMILY C — Comic / manga (inked) worlds
*Grounded worlds drawn with pen craft. Space is constructed, not just lit.*

#### C1 · American Modern Comic — "the grounded, kinetic inked reality"

**The world it builds.** A **dynamic, staged, high-readability universe** — the world of a premium modern American graphic novel: grounded architecture, believable space, and kinetic energy. The worldview is **clarity and motion over atmosphere**: everything is drawn *into* one coherent inked reality, bold and legible, built to move and to read at a glance.

**Space & scale.** **Solid construction** — real architecture and believable depth — staged dynamically with varied panel sizes, big impact panels, insets and splashes, and occasional **speed / impact lines**. Plain bold black gutters only; a clear left-to-right, top-to-bottom reading order guides the eye through the world.

**Light & atmosphere.** Crisp hard-edged **cel shadow** (2–3 tones) with a few clean highlights and tasteful spotting of black. The world reads bold and bright rather than hazy; drama comes from shape and staging, not from murk.

**Material & texture.** One unified **inked + cel-shaded** substance for the whole world — confident variable-width line (bold outer contours, finer interior lines) over flat color and cel shadow. Clean, bold, not sketchy, not noisy.

**Color as atmosphere.** Bold controlled comic color, high contrast, **neutral overall balance** — local colors can be saturated, but they never tint the whole world; clean near-neutral highlights.

**Figures within it.** Inked and cel-shaded in the same line weight and shadow language as the world and lit by the same light, so figures read as drawn into the panel, never stickered on.

```text
American modern comic / graphic-novel WORLD, 2:3 vertical, premium quality. #### C2 · Modern Seinen Manga — "the grounded, near-monochrome reality"
```

**The world it builds.** The most **observational, documentary** universe of the set: a mature seinen-manga world with realistic proportions, grounded architecture, real texture and cinematic camera — carried almost entirely by **ink, hatching and screentone**, with color kept to a thin subordinate whisper. The worldview is quiet naturalism and atmosphere-through-tone; the world feels *real and close*, without any decoration to romanticize it.

**Space & scale.** **Constructed realistic space** with cinematic framing and depth; varied panels, occasional slanted / diagonal gutters, big impact panels and splashes. Reading order may run **right-to-left**, kept consistent. Plain thin black gutters only — **absolutely no decorative or gold borders**; the plainness is a worldview choice.

**Light & atmosphere.** Volume and shadow built from **dense cross-hatching and screentone gradients**; motion from **speed lines and focus lines**; realistic surface detail where the light lands. Atmosphere comes from tone, not color.

**Material & texture.** Ink-driven high craft — bold clean outer contours, finer interior structure lines, screentone for form. The whole world is one inked-and-toned substance; premium seinen, not sketchy.

**Color as atmosphere.** Restrained, naturalistic, **near-monochrome**. Color is subordinate to line and tone — the inverse of the color-comic worlds.

**Figures within it.** Drawn in the same ink and screentone as the world and lit by the same light — realistic-leaning, integrated, never pasted.

## 3. The constant travelers (the fixed cast)

The two leads are the *only* thing that stays identical as the world changes around them — the reader's anchor across nine universes. Their **design is fixed; only the world's rendering language repaints them.**

- **Sun Wukong (孙悟空):** mustard-yellow monk robe, azure sash, deep-blue trousers, tarnished-gold circlet; stylized simian face, one small fang, lively eyes; adult, agile; never chibi, never a real monkey, never photoreal. One per panel, no clones. In every world he is *lit by that world's light* (or, in the DC world, the intentional toon guest that still catches its shadow).
- **The Iron Fan Princess (铁扇公主):** long black hair, a single ornate hairpin (angular where the world is deco); jade-green silk robe as her color anchor; sharp phoenix eyes, cool pale skin, red-tinted lips; regal bearing; the beauty focus with the top detail budget. Always dignified — any face-pulling belongs to a plain maid, never to her.

Design logic: identity = *silhouette + costume + color anchors + a consistent face*. Because these are pinned, each world is free to reinterpret the *surface* (paint, cel, ink, screentone) without the travelers drifting.

## 4. Workflow — designing any scene, world-first

Think **world before character, and world before content.** For any new panel or page, decide the world in this order; the cast and the beat drop in last.

1. **Name the universe.** Which of the nine worlds are you in, and what is its emotional register *for this beat*? (Mythic ceremony? Epic emptiness? Grave noir? Quiet realism?) This choice governs everything below.
2. **Set the space & scale.** Solid architecture, or silhouette-and-negative-space, or atmospheric depth? Where does scale put the viewer — dwarfed, level, intimate?
3. **Set the light logic.** One key light and deep shadow (cinematic worlds), hard flat shadow shapes (deco/animation worlds), cel or screentone tone (comic/manga worlds). Light is what makes the place *feel* like itself.
4. **Choose the material contract.** Unified (world and inhabitants one substance — most styles) or two-tier (a stylized guest in a richer world — DC, and the held-back detail tier of Minimalism). Decide it consciously; it is the biggest world decision.
5. **Set ornament vs restraint & framing.** Deco framing and lettering (Xianwuxia, DC), label cards (Organ Atlas), or plain black gutters and no decoration at all (American, Manga), or near-total emptiness (Minimalism).
6. **Tune the palette as atmosphere.** Pick the world's air from its profile; then apply the family-wide color discipline — neutral balance, gold local-only, costume colors never tinting the world.
7. **Place the camera & staging**, then **drop in the fixed cast** (§3) rendered in this world's language and lit by this world's light. Content and dialogue come from the story master.
8. **Spend the detail budget** on the beat's focal surface first, then the hero panel, everything else simplified into this world's shadow or flat shape — this keeps flat/minimal worlds stable and cinematic ones from turning noisy. If in-image text renders garbled, leave the balloon/label empty and add it in post.

The token order in the prompt follows the same logic: `[STYLE_HEADER + world blocks] → [space/light/framing blocks] → [fixed cast] → [layout for the page] → Negative:[the style's negative]`.
